How two's complement works

A fixed-width integer is stored as a pattern of bits with no separate sign field. Instead, the top bit doubles as a sign in signed mode: 0 for non-negative, 1 for negative. Flipping every bit and adding 1 negates a value, which is also why incrementing the all-ones pattern rolls it back to all zeros.

The same bits mean different things depending on how they're read. The byte 11111111 is 255 unsigned, but −1 signed. Because signed and unsigned ranges are offset rather than symmetric, negating the most negative signed value (like −128 for 8 bits) has no positive counterpart to land on -- it overflows and produces itself again.
